# F011(c): re-validating `CONFORM_BLEND_TIGHT` against a target that is real

2026-09-03. The last of F011's three blockers.

## Why the constant cannot simply be inherited

`CONFORM_BLEND_TIGHT = 0.3` is documented as *"How far a SKIN-HUGGING vert is
reeled back toward the standoff the author gave it. 0.3 means seven tenths of
the authored fit is deliberately not restored."*

That description is only true when the pass can SEE the authored standoff. On
the body-swap path at defaults it cannot: `src_body_n_p2` is the all-zero
stored normal array, so the authored standoff reads 0 for every vertex and
`blend_tight` is not "restore 30% of the author's fit" but **"pull each
skin-hugging vertex 30% of the way onto the skin"**. The knob was tuned in that
regime. Turning the producer fix on changes what the number MEANS, which is why
the audit asks for a re-validation rather than a straight flip.

The knob's own comment already says it is "the single largest lever on how
close to the original is this", and that the clearance knobs are not that lever
-- so if any constant needed re-checking after the fix, it is this one.

## Sweep

Four points, all with `SRC_NORMAL_FIX=1`, one harness, deployed exe, same mod
as F011(b). 0.30 is the shipped value and the reference.

Fired: each arm differs from the 0.30 arm on **56 of 74 NIFs**, and 0.15
differs from 0.70 on the same 56 -- so the arms are distinct from each other,
not merely distinct from the default. No caller passes `blend_tight`, so the
knob reaches all three `conform_to_source_standoff` call sites.

### Surface quality is FLAT across the whole range

| blend_tight | folded | inverted |
|---|---|---|
| 0.15 | 10220 | 360 |
| **0.30 (shipped)** | **10267** | **367** |
| 0.50 | 10255 | 393 |
| 0.70 | 10249 | 368 |

Spread: 47 folds across the range, against the **709** the producer fix itself
removes. The knob moves surface quality by about 7% of what turning the fix on
does, with no monotone trend -- it is noise at this resolution.

**So folds do not choose a value.** The re-validation question becomes purely
one of fit, which is the next section.

### SEAT ERROR against the author -- the metric the knob is named for

An author baseline IS available for this population, contrary to what the fold
census concluded. The garments are VFS-resolved from a BodySlide output mod, so
pairing against the converted mod's own folder finds only first-person meshes.
Resolving each output's data-relative path through the MO2 load order -- the way
the CONVERTER finds them -- pairs **234 shapes**.

Comparing raw vertex positions would be meaningless: the author's garment sits
on a CBBE body and ours on a UBE body, so they are supposed to differ. What
must match is the STANDOFF. So the author's cloth is measured against the CBBE
base body, ours against the UBE body, and the error is the difference between
those two distances, per vertex -- the same quantity the knob's own comment
names ("mean |our standoff - the author's|").

| arm | blend_tight | mean seat error | median |
|---|---|---|---|
| `ctrl` (producer fix OFF) | -- | **0.3806u** | 0.3169u |
| bt015 | 0.15 | 0.3112u | 0.2753u |
| **fix (shipped)** | **0.30** | **0.3085u** | **0.2726u** |
| bt050 | 0.50 | 0.3077u | 0.2711u |
| bt070 | 0.70 | 0.3060u | 0.2700u |

**1. The producer fix cuts seat error by 19%** (0.3806 -> 0.3085u), measured
against the author's own meshes. Every other number in this campaign is a
side-effect metric -- folds, clipping, standoff-from-body. This is the thing
F011 is actually about: does the garment end up where its author put it. It
does, and by a wide margin.

**2. The knob is nearly inert: 1.7% across a 4.7x range.** 0.15 -> 0.70 moves
seat error 0.3112 -> 0.3060u. The trend is monotone (more restoration = closer
to the author, as designed) and consistent across four points, so it is real --
but it is 0.005u against the fix's own 0.072u.

## Verdict on F011(c)

**0.30 needs no retune.** It sits on a plateau: the best point measured buys
0.0025u (0.8%) over it, and buying that means restoring more authored standoff,
which is the direction that costs clipping. There is no evidence for moving it
and a small reason not to.

The re-validation the audit asked for is therefore satisfied in the useful
direction: the constant survives its target becoming real.

### One claim in the constant's comment does NOT survive

The comment calls `CONFORM_BLEND_TIGHT` *"the single largest lever on 'how
close to the original is this'"*. With the producer fix ON that is false by the
comment's own metric -- the knob moves seat error 1.7% while the fix moves it
19%. After the fix, the residual 0.31u is NOT dominated by how much of the
authored standoff conform restores; it comes from somewhere else, and that is a
lead worth having.

Stated precisely, because the distinction matters: this measures the knob with
the fix ON. In the SHIPPED regime (fix off, target identically zero) the knob
may well be the large lever the comment describes -- pulling 30% of the way
toward zero is a big move when zero is wrong. That case was not swept here, so
the comment is qualified rather than contradicted.

### Clip and standoff across the sweep -- the trade is in the TAIL

252 shapes, paired mask, 0.30 as reference:

| blend | standoff median | p90 | clip median | **clip worst** | shapes moved |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.15 | +0.0002u | +0.019 | +0.0000 | +0.131 | 65/252 |
| **0.30** | reference | | | | |
| 0.50 | -0.0000u | +0.010 | +0.0000 | +0.213 | 79/252 |
| 0.70 | -0.0006u | +0.019 | +0.0000 | **+0.311** | 98/252 |

Medians are flat to four decimal places -- consistent with the seat-error
result, and with folds. What moves is the TAIL: the worst single clipping
regression grows monotonically as the knob rises (0.131 -> 0.213 -> 0.311
pct-pt), and so does the number of shapes touched (65 -> 79 -> 98).

That is exactly the trade the mechanism predicts. Restoring more of the
authored standoff lifts cloth off the body, and where the author's own fit was
looser than the UBE body needs, lifting it further lets skin through.

**So 0.70 buys 0.0025u of seat error and costs +0.311 pct-pt on the worst
shape.** Two-thousandths of a unit closer to the author, against a third of a
percentage point more skin showing on the piece that can least afford it. The
sweep does not merely fail to justify raising the knob -- it argues against it.

## Verdict, with the fit numbers in

**`CONFORM_BLEND_TIGHT` stays at 0.30.** F011(c) is answered: the constant
survives its target becoming real, on the metric it is named for (seat error),
on surface quality (flat), and on fit (medians flat, tail worsening upward).
